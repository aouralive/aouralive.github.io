Phone live stream setup: lighting, angle and sound made simple

You do not need a studio to look and sound good on a live. A phone, a window or a lamp, and a few minutes of preparation make a clear difference. Here is how to set everything up at home without spending much.

Light: in front of you, never behind

Light is what makes the biggest difference. During the day, sit facing a window so the daylight falls on your face. Never sit with a window or a bright lamp behind you: the camera will darken your face.

At night, place a lamp or a ring light in front of you, slightly above eye level. Avoid a single bulb straight above your head, which casts shadows under the eyes. Before each live, wipe the front camera lens with a soft cloth; a greasy lens makes the whole image blurry.

Phone position and framing

Do not hold the phone in your hand: the image shakes and your arm gets tired. A cheap phone stand or small tripod is enough, or lean the phone against a stack of books.

Put the camera at the height of your eyes, at about one arm's length. Keep the phone vertical and frame your head and shoulders with a little space above your head. A camera placed too low gives an unflattering angle and makes it harder for viewers to connect with you.

Clear sound

Viewers forgive an average picture faster than bad sound. Choose the quietest room, close the windows and turn off the TV, fans or washing machine during the live.

Earphones with a built-in microphone help a lot, especially in a noisy home. Rooms with curtains, a carpet or a sofa echo less than empty rooms. Record a 20-second test video and listen to it before your first live.

Stable internet and battery

A live that freezes loses viewers. Use Wi-Fi when you can and sit close to the router; if you use mobile data, check that the signal is strong where you sit.

Start with a full battery or keep the charger plugged in, because live video uses a lot of power. Close other apps and turn on Do Not Disturb so calls and notifications do not interrupt you or appear on screen.

A simple, tidy background

A clean, simple background looks better than a busy one. One or two touches, like a plant, a small light or a cushion, are enough to give your space a personality.

Check that nothing personal is visible: letters with your address, documents or family photos. Your background should show your style, not your private life.

A 2-minute check before every live

Before you press start: light in front of you, lens clean, phone stable at eye level, room quiet, battery charged, Wi-Fi on, Do Not Disturb active, background tidy.

Repeating this short routine each time makes your lives look regular and professional, and lets you focus on what matters most: talking with your viewers.

Common questions

Do I need expensive equipment to go live?

No. A recent phone is enough. A cheap stand and a lamp or ring light already improve the result a lot.

What is the best light for a live at night?

A lamp or ring light placed in front of you, slightly above eye level, with no strong light behind you.
