Live streaming safety: how to protect your privacy

Live streaming is a fun way to meet people from everywhere, but your privacy and safety always come first. This guide gives you simple habits that protect you before, during and after a live, without taking the fun out of chatting with viewers.

Never share your personal details

Don't give your home address, neighbourhood or exact city, your phone number, the name of your school, university or workplace, or your full name. Small details can be pieced together to find out where you are.

Use a simple stage name on your profile instead of your real one, and don't link your personal social media accounts. If someone asks "where exactly are you from?", naming your country is enough, or kindly change the subject.

Check what your camera shows behind you

Before going live, look at your frame the way a viewer will see it: is there a window facing the street or a shop sign? An envelope or bill with your address, family photos, a school or work uniform? Move all of that out of view.

A plain background, like a bare wall or a curtain, is the safest choice. Check your phone settings and turn off location sharing for apps that don't need it, and remember that any viewer can take a screenshot, so never show anything you wouldn't want recorded.

Keep conversations inside the app

Some people will ask for your WhatsApp number or a private account "to talk more". The safest answer is a polite no, because the app's protection and reporting tools only work inside the app.

Never send private photos, never send money or bank details, and don't believe anyone who promises big gifts in exchange for a favour. These are well-known scams, and respectful viewers don't ask for any of this.

Use block and report without hesitating

If someone is rude, keeps asking personal questions or makes inappropriate requests, don't argue. Open their profile from the live and use the block or report option the app provides, then calmly carry on with your stream.

You don't have to please everyone, and no gift buys the right to cross your limits. If the harassment keeps happening, tell the agency team you signed up through so they can help you handle it.

Protect your account from being stolen

Choose a strong password you don't use anywhere else, and turn on two-step verification if it is available. Never give the verification code you receive by text message to anyone, even if they say they are from support.

Messages asking you to "confirm your account" through an outside link are usually scams. Always log in through the app itself, and review its privacy settings from time to time.

Set your boundaries and stick to them

Decide in advance what you are and aren't comfortable with on a live, and stay with it. You can always say no to a request, and you can end a live any time you feel uncomfortable. The app's rules forbid inappropriate content, and following them protects both you and your account.

A good live is built on conversation, laughter and respectful interaction, and most viewers appreciate a confident host who knows her limits.

Common questions

Do I have to use my real name?

No. You can use a stage name on your profile, which is safer for your privacy.

What should I do if a viewer harasses me?

Don't argue. Use the block or report option in the app, and tell the agency team if it keeps happening.

Who can sign up as a host?
