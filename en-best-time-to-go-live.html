Best time to go live: how to get and keep more viewers

The same live can get a handful of viewers or a full room, just because of the hour you press start. 🕗 The good news? Timing is something you can learn fast. Here is when to go live, how long to stay, and how to make people stay with you once they arrive.

Go live when your viewers are free

The busiest hours on most live-streaming apps are evenings, roughly from 8 pm to midnight, when people are home after work or school. Weekends, especially Friday and Saturday nights, are usually stronger than weekday mornings.

Think about where your viewers live, not only where you live. If many of them are in another time zone, a late-afternoon live for you can be prime time for them. Ask in your lives: "Where are you watching from? What time is it there?" Their answers tell you a lot.

Choose a fixed schedule and share it

Regular times build habits. Pick 3 or 4 days a week and keep the same hour, for example Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Sunday at 9 pm. Your followers quickly learn when to find you.

Write your schedule in your profile bio and repeat it at the end of every live: "See you Wednesday at 9! 💜". A schedule you can keep for months is better than a perfect one you drop after a week.

How long should a live last?

Viewers discover a live little by little, so very short lives rarely have time to grow. Aim for at least 60 to 90 minutes, and longer if the room is lively and you feel good.

If you have little time, two focused one-hour lives at strong hours beat one tired three-hour live at a quiet hour. Keep water nearby and take a 1-minute stretch break if you need it, while telling viewers you will be right back.

The first 5 minutes decide everything

New visitors decide in seconds whether to stay. Start with energy: smile, say hello out loud and explain what today's live is about. Never sit silently waiting for people to arrive.

Greet every person who joins by name and ask a simple question they can answer quickly, like "Tea or coffee tonight?" ☕ A viewer who types one message is much more likely to stay.

Keep the room alive with games and PK

Prepare 3 or 4 topics or mini-games before you start: a quick quiz, guessing a song, "this or that" questions, or your goal for the week. When the chat gets quiet, you already know what to say next.

PK challenges with other hosts are one of the best ways to meet new viewers, and they work best at peak hours when many people are online. Thank every gift and every message by name: people come back where they feel noticed. 🎁

Learn from every live

After each live, write down three things: the start time, how many viewers you had at the peak, and what made the chat most active. It takes one minute.

After two weeks, look at your notes: you will see which days and hours work best for you. Keep the winners, test one new time slot, and keep going. Most successful hosts started small and grew step by step. 🚀

Common questions

What is the best time to go live?

Usually evenings from about 8 pm to midnight and weekend nights, but the best time depends on where your viewers live. Test different hours and keep the ones that work.

How many times a week should I go live?

Three to four lives a week at fixed times is a great start. Being regular matters more than streaming every day.
